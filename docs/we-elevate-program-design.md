## 2. Program Pillars

### Work (Grounding & Skills)
**Purpose:** Physical work that's therapeutic, builds routine, and contributes to the community.

**Intake Process:**
- During intake, present a list of available roles/jobs
- Let each resident choose based on interest and strengths
- They can request a change later if unhappy
- Goal: ownership and agency from day one

**Roles/Options:**
- Farm: Growing vegetables, herbs, eggs (chickens)
- Garden: Flowers, therapeutic plots
- Maintenance: Property upkeep, basic repairs
- Kitchen: Meal prep, cooking rotations
- Agritourism: Campground host, farm stand, seasonal events
- Social enterprise: Farmers market, crafts, products

**Skills learned:**
- Physical fitness & discipline
- Horticulture & agriculture
- Basic construction/maintenance
- Food prep & nutrition
- Small business operations

### Workshop Instructors (The "Help You, Help Us" Model)

Rather than paying for instructors, we partner with organizations and students who need volunteer/service hours for their certifications or degrees.

**Potential Partners:**
- **Nursing programs** (local colleges) — health workshops, first aid
- **Counseling/psychology programs** — peer support training, stress management
- **Education programs** — tutoring, GED support
- **Social work programs** — case management, resources
- **Culinary schools** — cooking & nutrition
- **Trade schools** — basic repairs, maintenance skills
- **Barber/beauty schools** — grooming, self-care
- **Community colleges** — computer basics, job readiness
- **Church groups** — spiritual support, life skills

**How it works:**
1. Reach out to local programs (email, in-person)
2. Explain the partnership: they provide instructors, students get hours
3. Create a schedule that works for both sides
4. Build long-term relationships with reliable partners

**Benefits:**
- Free or low-cost workshops
- Fresh perspectives and energy
- Professional connections for residents
- Community goodwill

**Initial outreach targets:**
- [ ] Local community college (nursing, counseling, business)
- [ ] Trade school (local or online)
- [ ] Churches (volunteer speakers)
- [ ] County extension office (financial literacy, gardening)
- [ ] Local businesses (job skills, industry experts)

## 6. Phases & Graduation

### Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1–3)
**Goals:**
- Settle in, orientation complete
- Assessment done (strengths, gaps, goals)
- Housing assignment stable
- Financial literacy basics started
- Work routine established

**Milestone:** Phase 1 completion meeting with case manager

---

### Phase 2: Building (Months 3–12)
**Goals:**
- Enrolled in job training or education
- Financial literacy modules complete
- Work skills building
- Credit repair underway (if applicable)
- Savings goal: 3 months living expenses

**Milestone:** Employment secured or enrollment confirmed for Phase 3

---

### Phase 3: Transition (Months 12–18+)
**Goals:**
- Full-time employment or stable enrollment
- Housing search underway
- Life skills reinforced
- Graduation plan in place
- Support network established (church, community, peers)

**Graduation Requirements:**
- Employment or enrollment (minimum 20 hrs/week)
- 3 months of living expenses saved
- Housing plan approved by case manager
- Completion of financial literacy program
- Exit interview

## 8. House Policies (Draft)

### Drugs & Alcohol
- We are **not a rehab facility** — we do not control personal choices
- **No illegal activity on property**
- If behavior affects others or prevents participation, a conversation + consequence follows
- Goal: support, not punishment — but safety and community come first

### Visitation
- Day visits only, during designated hours (10 AM – 6 PM)
- **No overnight visitors** — liability and safety
- All visitors check in with staff
- Partners: encourage waiting until after graduation; if in relationship, case manager approval + boundaries

### Program Participation
- Expected to attend morning gathering, work block, workshops
- Personal time respected, but isolation concerning
- If someone is struggling, approach with care first

### Consequences
1. Conversation (check-in)
2. Written warning
3. Probation (30 days to course-correct)
4. Discharge — only after exhaustive attempts

## 10. Intake Process

### Who Qualifies (Admission Criteria)

**Target Population:**
- Women 18+
- Going through divorce OR widowed
- Financially stranded (no income or <$1,000/month)
- No other support system
- **Children NOT required** — we accept women with or without dependents

**Exclusions (referrer should screen for):**
- Women in immediate danger (refer to DV shelters)
- Severe addiction requiring detox (refer to treatment)
- Severe mental health crisis (refer to appropriate care)
- Demonstrated history of violence or harm

**Requirements:**
- Willingness to participate in program
- **Referral from approved partner only** (no self-referrals)
- **Referrer provides 3 personal references** for the referred person
- No background check — trust the referring partner's vetting

---

### The Intake Journey

**Step 1: Referral Received**
- Partner organization sends referral form
- Brief intake info: situation, income, children, timeline
- Check: meets basic criteria?

**Step 2: Screening Call**
- 15–20 minute phone call with Program Coordinator (Shelby)
- Learn more about their story, needs, goals
- Explain the program, expectations, culture
- Answer their questions

**Step 3: In-Person Interview**
- Come for a tour, meet staff
- More in-depth conversation
- Assess: Is this the right fit? Are they ready?
- No pressure — we both decide

**Step 4: Acceptance & Move-In**
- If accepted: set move-in date
- Move-in day: orientation, cabin assignment, meet the community
- Present job/role options — they choose
- Goal: settled within 48 hours
