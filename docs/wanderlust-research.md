## 1. Similar Programs & Organizations

### What's Already Out There

| Organization | Location | Model | What They Offer | Gap/What's Missing |
|--------------|----------|-------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Domestic Violence Shelters** | Nationwide | Crisis short-term (30-90 days) | Safety, emergency housing | Not for financially stranded (no abuse) |
| **Homeless Shelters** | Nationwide | Emergency | Overnight housing, meals | No programs, no long-term |
| **Halfway Houses** | Nationwide | Post-treatment/incarceration | Structured, sober living | Not for divorce/widowhood |
| **Oxford House** | National | Peer-run sober homes | Self-governed, affordable | No program, not specific to women in transition |
| **Women for Women International** | 8 countries | Year-long program | Skills training, health education, business skills, rights education | International (war/conflict), not US-based housing |
| **Faith-Based Transitional Housing** | Varies | Religious ministry | Free/sliding scale housing | Often addiction-focused, not comprehensive |
| **Single Mother Housing** | Varies | Income-based | Housing assistance | Not focused on divorce/widowhood specifically |

### Types of Existing Programs

1. **Domestic Violence Shelters** — Immediate safety, short-term (30-90 days)
2. **Homeless Shelters** — Emergency housing, no program
3. **Halfway Houses** — Post-rehab, post-incarceration focus
4. **Oxford House** — Peer-run, self-governed, sober living
5. **Single Mother Housing** — Income-based, not specific to divorce/widowhood
6. **Faith-Based** — Free or sliding scale, often ministry-focused
7. **Women for Women International** — Skills training, group support (but international)

### Your Unique Positioning

*What makes Wanderlust & Co. different?*

- **Target population**: Women economically stranded after years of marriage (not crisis DV, not homeless, not post-addiction)
- **Full-spectrum**: Housing + Farm + Workshops + Homeschool + Healing (emotional, spiritual, confidence)
- **Long-term**: Not a 30-day fix, but a rebuild
- **Farm as therapy**: Physical work, grounding, connection to earth
- **Moms lead homeschool**: Gives purpose and teaching opportunity
- **Holistic healing**: Confidence building, spiritual work, emotional processing
- **Skills for independence**: Real job skills, financial literacy

**The Gap**: There is no existing program that specifically serves housewives/stay-at-home mothers who were financially dependent and now find themselves divorced or widowed with no path forward. This is the missing middle — too stable for DV shelters, too broke for regular housing, too invisible for any assistance.

### Investor Funding — How It Works

**What it is:**
Individual wealthy people (angel investors) or groups who put money into projects they believe in. Often in exchange for some ownership (equity) or as a loan.

**For your type of project:**
- Social impact investors — care about "doing good" not just returns
- Faith-based investors — churches, religious organizations
- Angel networks — groups of investors who meet to hear pitches

**Common approaches:**

1. **Investor dinner/drop-in**
   - Invite 5-10 potential investors to a meal
   - Share your vision and story
   - Hand out one-page summaries
   - Ask if they'd like to learn more

2. **Pitch events**
   - Some communities host "pitch nights"
   - You present to a room of investors
   - Usually 5-10 minutes, sometimes a "shark tank" style

3. **One-on-one meetings**
   - Meet individually with an interested investor
   - More personal, deeper conversation

4. **Formal pitch deck**
   - Slide presentation with your business plan
   - Financial projections
   - How their investment helps

**What investors want to see:**
- Clear vision and passion (you have this)
- Market need (the gap you're filling)
- How their money creates impact
- Realistic plan (numbers don't have to be perfect)
- You (they invest in YOU)

**Tips:**
- Start with people who know you/your story
- Practice your pitch
- Have a one-page summary ready
- Don't ask for too much at first — start the relationship
- Be willing to explain, not everyone knows this space

**Where to find investors:**
- Church communities
- Chamber of Commerce events
- Local business networking groups
- Social media (LinkedIn, Twitter)
- Referrals from people you know

### Questions
- Will residents pay anything?
- Is this a nonprofit (501c3) or for-profit?
- What covers operating costs?

### Regenerative Practices

**Why regenerative matters:**
- Soil health → better yields
- Sustainability → less input over time
- Teaching residents → valuable skills
- Stewardship → aligns with healing philosophy

#### Practices to Implement

**1. Rotational Grazing**
- Move livestock between pasture sections
- Prevents overgrazing
- Natural fertilization
- Improves soil health

**2. Cover Cropping**
- Plant off-season crops to protect soil
- Nitrogen fixation (clover, peas)
- Organic matter building

**3. Composting**
- Farm waste → nutrient-rich soil
- Kitchen scraps → compost
- Manure management

**4. Mulching**
- Retain moisture
- Suppress weeds
- Soil temperature regulation

**5. Rainwater Collection**
- Capture water for irrigation
- Reduce municipal water dependence
- Drought resilience

**6. No-Till / Low-Till**
- Preserve soil structure
- Reduce erosion
- Support soil microbiology

**7. Integrated Pest Management**
- Natural pest control
- Companion planting
- Pollinator support (bees)
